## Verus-SpecGym: An Agentic Environment for Evaluating Specification Autoformalization
- link: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.26457
- signal: citations 6 | sources: openalex
- matched: agent, agentic, environment, evaluation, benchmark, verifier, verification, llm judge

AI coding agents are increasingly used to write real-world software, but ensuring that their outputs are correct remains a fundamental challenge. Formal verification offers a promising path: an agent generates code together with a machine-checked proof, guaranteeing that the code satisfies a formal specification. However, there is no guarantee that the formal spec itself matches the user's intent. In this work, we study specification autoformalization: whether LLM agents can translate informal programming problems into faithful formal specifications. We introduce Verus-SpecBench, a benchmark of 581 spec-writing tasks derived from Codeforces problems targeting Verus, a verifier for Rust, and Verus-SpecGym, an agentic environment in which models interact with Verus, bash, & the filesystem to develop these specs. The central challenge is evaluation: expert-written reference specs are expensive to write, & LLM judges can miss subtle mistakes. We address this by (a) extending Verus's exec_spec mechanism so that generated specs can be executed as Rust code, & (b) testing them against official Codeforces tests & adversarial cases extracted from Codeforces "hacks", which are edge cases written by competitors to break incorrect solutions. On Verus-SpecBench, the strongest model, Gemini 3.1 Pro, solves 77.8% of tasks, other frontier models solve 51.1--57.8% & OSS models reach only 21.5--25.5%. Our analysis of failure modes shows that model-generated specs can omit important input assumptions, accept incorrect outputs, & reject valid ones. We also find that LLM-as-a-judge evaluation misses 26% of the failures our evaluator catches. Overall, our results suggest that spec autoformalization is within reach for frontier agents but remains brittle even on problems where they can already generate correct code. The code, data, & logs can be found at https://github.com/formal-verif-is-cool/verus-spec-gym

## ARE: Scaling Up Agent Environments and Evaluations
- link: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2509.17158
- signal: citations 66 | sources: openalex
- matched: agent, agentic, environment, evaluation, benchmark, verifier, synthetic, orchestration

We introduce Meta Agents Research Environments (ARE), a research platform for scalable creation of environments, integration of synthetic or real applications, and execution of agentic orchestrations. ARE provides simple abstractions to build complex and diverse environments, each with their own rules, tools, content, and verifiers, helping to bridge the gap between model development and real-world deployment. We also propose Gaia2, a benchmark built in ARE and designed to measure general agent capabilities. Beyond search and execution, Gaia2 requires agents to handle ambiguities and noise, adapt to dynamic environments, collaborate with other agents, and operate under temporal constraints. Unlike prior benchmarks, Gaia2 runs asynchronously, surfacing new failure modes that are invisible in static settings. Our experiments show that no system dominates across the intelligence spectrum: stronger reasoning often comes at the cost of efficiency, and budget scaling curves plateau, highlighting the need for new architectures and adaptive compute strategies. Perhaps more importantly, ARE abstractions enable continuous extension of Gaia2 to other environments, empowering the community to rapidly create new benchmarks tailored to their domains. In AI's second half, progress increasingly depends on defining meaningful tasks and robust evaluations to drive frontier capabilities forward.

## AJ-Bench: Benchmarking Agent-as-a-Judge for Environment-Aware Evaluation
- link: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2604.18240
- signal: citations 12 | sources: openalex
- matched: agent, environment, evaluation, benchmark, verifier, verification, llm-as-a-judge

As reinforcement learning continues to scale the training of large language model-based agents, reliably verifying agent behaviors in complex environments has become increasingly challenging. Existing approaches rely on rule-based verifiers or LLM-as-a-Judge models, which struggle to generalize beyond narrow domains. Agent-as-a-Judge addresses this limitation by actively interacting with environments and tools to acquire verifiable evidence, yet its capabilities remain underexplored. We introduce a benchmark AJ-Bench to systematically evaluate Agent-as-a-Judge across three domains-search, data systems, and graphical user interfaces-comprising 155 tasks and 516 annotated trajectories. The benchmark comprehensively assesses judge agents' abilities in information acquisition, state verification, and process verification. Experiments demonstrate consistent performance gains over LLM-as-a-Judge baselines, while also revealing substantial open challenges in agent-based verification. Our data and code are available at https://aj-bench.github.io/.

## Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? Rethinking Criteria Exposure in Embodied Self-Improvement
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08318
- signal: upvotes 21 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, benchmark, verifier, verification, simulation, self-improve

Self-improving embodied agents are increasingly being developed and compared on robot simulation benchmarks. Several recent works expose the benchmark's success criteria to the learning agent, a practice we call Criteria Exposure (CE). These criteria, originally written to verify policies trained by imitation learning, often demand more than the corresponding language instructions, so a score from such a verifier may not faithfully reflect whether the instructed task was completed, especially for robot-control programs written by an agent. As embodied coding agents report ever higher benchmark scores, a natural question arises: when a coding agent is exposed to the benchmark's criteria, does it generate code that better performs the instructed task, or code that simply overfits to the verification criteria? We conduct controlled experiments with a self-improving agent with and without CE, and find that with CE the agent quickly produces code with a higher success rate under the given criteria, but this advantage disappears when the criteria are changed slightly without departing from the original instructions. More importantly, the skills the agent develops under CE transfer poorly across tasks. We further propose instruction-faithful criteria and show that they better reflect the performance of agent-generated programs.

Across agentic benchmarks,  achieves higher held-out performance than baseline methods. The results suggest that agent training should optimize not only the actions in the current rollout, but also the model-written experience that future rollouts inherit.

## Envisioning Agentic Robotics: A New Era of Robotic Systems
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18359
- signal: upvotes 85 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, agentic, environment, evaluation, benchmark

The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.

## SO-Arena: Benchmarking the Capability Spectrum and Reliability of System One Models
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15777
- signal: upvotes 85 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: sandbox, evaluation, benchmark, verification, reliability

System One models promise fast, general-purpose decision-making without lengthy token generation. However, understanding their capability spectrum and decision reliability requires more than aggregate performance on current benchmarks. Existing evaluations often provide limited insight into the breadth of model capabilities and the reliability of their decisions. We introduce SO-Arena, a benchmark characterizing the capability spectrum and decision reliability of System One models across 11 domains and five capability families. For the capability spectrum, SO-Arena offers evidence-grounded evaluation through reliable sources and manual verification of reference labels against model-visible inputs; fine-grained capability assessment through domain-specific difficulty criteria; and realistic interactive evaluation, where models iteratively execute actions in sandboxes. Reasoning-intensive tasks further enable system one-two comparison. Beyond accuracy, SO-Arena assesses decision reliability through two complementary measures: decision stability under controlled option-set changes and missing-answer recognition when no valid option is available. Evaluations across 21 models reveal that the benefits of scaling and additional reasoning vary substantially across domains. More importantly, strong overall performance does not necessarily imply reliable decision-making: the best-performing System One model ranks only seventh in reliability. Together, these findings highlight the need to characterize capability spectrum and decision reliability as complementary dimensions, providing a systematic view of the strengths and limitations of System One models.

## VibePrevisBench: Evaluating Agentic Coding for 4D Scene Generation in Blender
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15789
- signal: upvotes 80 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, agentic, evaluation, benchmark, verification

Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these videos and the long, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene reconstruction: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. Its 242 tasks span four input kinds, including scripts, photos, continuations, and reference videos. Each delivery is scored along two evaluation dimensions. Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the code and compares the exported 4D scene with hidden ground truth facts in nine dimensions. VLM-based Screen Check asks a VLM judge whether the facts that pass can be recognized in the proxy video. We analyze the evaluation results and trajectories of 11 frontier coding agents and identify three key observations. First, multimodal understanding separates the agents, especially in understanding space and camera motion. Second, all agents seem to already master basic camera work and staging, and fail at challenging requests. Third, agents inspect their own renders but fail to correct their errors. Agents thus need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief.

## SEAGym: An Evaluation Environment for Self-Evolving LLM Agents
- link: https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.17546
- signal: citations 19 | sources: openalex
- matched: agent, environment, evaluation, benchmark, reliability

Self-evolving LLM-based agents improve mainly by changing their agent harness: the structured execution layer around a base model, including prompts, memory, tools, middleware, runtime state, and the model-tool interaction loop. Existing evaluations often reduce this process to isolated task scores or a single sequential curve, obscuring whether an update produces reusable improvement, overfits recent tasks, increases cost, or harms older behavior. We introduce SEAGym, an evaluation environment for measuring agent harness updates across training, validation, test, replay, and cost records. SEAGym turns Harbor-compatible benchmarks into dynamic self-evolution task sources with train batches, frozen update-validation, held-out ID and OOD transfer views, replay diagnostics, and saved snapshot and metric records. Instantiating SEAGym on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and HLE, we compare ACE, TF-GRPO, and AHE under a shared epoch/batch protocol. The results show that these evaluation views provide complementary signals about the evolution process: frequent updates may fail to improve held-out performance, useful intermediate snapshots may collapse later, and source diversity and model backend can affect harness reliability.

## JFinBench: A Continuously Refreshable Japanese Financial Benchmark from the Latest Certification Exams
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18130
- signal: upvotes 8 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, agentic, evaluation, benchmark, human-in-the-loop

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to finance, creating a need for benchmarks that evaluate their capabilities across financial systems with different regulations and practices. However, existing financial benchmarks provide limited coverage of country-specific financial systems, and they are difficult to keep up to date as released questions leak into training data. We introduce JFinBench, a Japanese financial benchmark comprising 321 examples from the 2026 editions of six regularly administered certification exams covering accounting, tax, financial planning, and banking. To construct it, we develop a human-in-the-loop agentic pipeline in which a coding agent writes exam-specific conversion code and a review page, while a human verifies every example and feeds corrections back to the agent. When models can additionally use a terminal and web access, they retrieve the knowledge they lack and every model improves. The gap narrows but about 4 points remain, and the ranking of models stays largely the same, suggesting that knowledge held in the model itself remains valuable even with tools. We release the dataset, the evaluation code, and the construction pipeline.

## Agent-R^2: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219
- signal: upvotes 8 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, agentic, environment, tool use, evaluation

Large language models have made rapid progress in agentic reasoning and tool use, raising expectations for agents that can take over realistic productivity work from humans. A key bottleneck is the lack of scalable post-training data that reflects real work intent, authentic file environments, and deliverable-level evaluation. To close this gap, we introduce Agent-R^2, a real-source data synthesis framework that scales training data for realistic productivity work. ## DeepStress: Stress-Testing Deep Search Agents
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13920
- signal: upvotes 7 | sources: hf-daily
- matched: agent, environment, benchmark, synthetic, reliability

While search agents demonstrate impressive capabilities in multi-step question answering, their robustness to poor-quality evidence remains under-explored. Such evidence is rare in benchmark corpora, and resulting failures are hard to reproduce. We propose DeepStress, a dynamic stress-testing framework that subjects agents to degraded evidence at controlled rates. It replaces the retrieval module with a synthetic environment where documents are generated dynamically for each query, under independent control of their trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality. Disease diagnosis is a key application of large language model (LLM) agents in healthcare.
