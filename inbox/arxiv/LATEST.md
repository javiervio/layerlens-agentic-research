## Ask Your Advisor: Learning When to Consult Stronger Models in Agentic Systems
- arxiv_id: 2610.13870v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Pascal Sowietzki, Steffen Holter, Yannick Metz, Emil Kasper, Raphael Baur, Mennatallah El-Assady
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13870v1

To balance task performance and computational cost, agentic workflows can use a lower-cost model for execution and selectively consult a stronger model. In such executor-advisor systems, the executor decides when to seek advice, making escalation depend on self-assessment, although current language models struggle to detect where their own reasoning fails. The need for stronger reasoning emerges during execution and varies across workflow phases. We introduce ARIA, a meta-controller that learns when to consult by balancing task success against consultation cost. Our approach formulates consultation as a sequential resource-allocation problem. At workflow contact points, ARIA maps execution evidence to a compact state abstraction and selects SILENT or CONSULT, with action values learned by Monte Carlo control from cost-penalized outcomes. Across evaluated tasks of SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.1, and $τ^2$-bench, ARIA recovers 44-81% of the executor-advisor success gap at less than half the advisor model's cost. ARIA achieves the best observed success-cost trade-off among ten other evaluated conditions, including routing and cascading methods, fixed and prompted consultation, and random placement at matched advice volume. The policies are compact and inspectable, with a single consultation-price parameter governing the success-cost trade-off without retraining either underlying model.

## MARGoS: A Configuration-Driven Platform for Reproducible MARL Experiments in ARGoS
- arxiv_id: 2610.13772v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA

- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13772v1

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has become a prominent research direction, particularly within collective robotics, where the ARGoS simulator is widely used to study emergent swarm behavior. Yet despite this growth, systematic support for reuse and reproducibility in MARL-based swarm robotics remains limited and insufficiently standardized: a single experiment is assembled from manually aligned simulator files, training scripts, configuration values, paths, seeds, and environment details, so reuse and reproduction stay fragile even within one research group. ## OpenSocialJax: Evaluating Social Adaptation in Sequential Social Dilemmas with Open-Ended Environmental Dynamics
- arxiv_id: 2610.13710v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Zihao Guo, Meng Fang, Joel Z. Leibo, Yali Du
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13710v1

Sequential social dilemma benchmarks capture diverse social interactions, but rarely test whether agents can infer hidden environmental rules through repeated interaction while adapting to other agents. We introduce \textbf{OpenSocialJax}, which extends Clean Up and Commons Harvest across an open-ended distribution of latent environment configurations, systematically varying spatial layouts and hidden rules governing tool crafting, payoffs, and resource dynamics. Each episode samples one configuration from tens of millions of possibilities and holds it fixed across five trials. We evaluate eight large language models, including three closed source models and five open source models. Our evaluation tests whether models can discover hidden rules and adapt to other agents in homogeneous and mixed teams. We find that rule discovery often improves performance but does not reliably sustain cooperation, as the cleaning burden becomes concentrated among fewer agents in OpenCleanup and every team eventually exhausts the shared resource in OpenHarvest. In our mixed arena, one cooperative agent was able to sustain the public good in OpenCleanup at a cost to its own return, but could not prevent resource depletion in OpenHarvest. Together, these results highlight the difficulty of adapting jointly to unfamiliar environmental dynamics and the changing behavior of other agents.

## Cooperative Aerial Transportation of a Payload with a Sliding Cable Attachment
- arxiv_id: 2610.13665v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Sebastian Tilaguy Lezama, Diego S D'Antonio
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13665v1

This paper presents a dual-quadrotor hook- suspended transport system for cooperative aerial manipulation of a payload that is not rigidly attached to a fixed point along the cable. Two quadrotors are connected by a single inextensible cable that passes through a frictionless hook mounted on the payload, allowing the cable to slide between its two segments. This introduces a cable-distribution variable that couples the quadrotor motion, cable geometry, and payload dynamics. A feasible reference-generation framework is formulated using the desired payload pose, cable distribution, and system shape while satisfying the cable-length constraint and elliptical workspace. ## A Multi-Agent LLM System for Automated Tuning of Specialized Neural Network Models
- arxiv_id: 2610.13663v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA

- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13663v1

Many machine learning approaches are two-level: an expert selects a class of methods, within this class the choice of key components is critical and usually manual. This paper addresses the automatic tuning of these components and proposes a framework transferable across models of different nature. The multi-agent large language model (LLM) framework has three roles---analyst, coder, and recorder~---with an experiment log and a sandboxed execution environment. Unlike systems that optimize the entire ML script or search architectures in a discrete space, the framework operates over an open but semantically narrow space: the agent generates code only for the specified components, leaving the overall network structure unchanged. ## Limit-sure Winning in Team Games without Shared Randomness
- arxiv_id: 2610.13569v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Léonard Brice, Thomas A. Henzinger, Alipasha Montaseri, Ali Shafiee, K. S. Thejaswini
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13569v1

We study concurrent stochastic games in which a team of agents plays against an adversarial environment. If the agents could access a shared source of randomness, then these games reduce to two-player games. Despite being common in the literature on coalition games, this assumption does not accurately model real-world systems. In this paper, we consider the setting where the agents only have access to individual sources of randomness, and must satisfy a given reachability or safety objective. The limit-sure problem asks whether the team can ensure that the objective is satisfied with probability at least $1-λ$ for every $λ>0$. For reachability objectives, we show that this problem is $\mathsf{NP}$-complete. For safety objectives, we show that limit-sure winning coincides with almost-sure winning, which is known to lie in $\mathsf{P}$. As a corollary of these results, we conclude that model checking of the qualitative fragment of the logic Individually Randomised ATL -- introduced in the recent work of Brice et al. -- is $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{NP}}$-complete.

## Collective Autonomous Vehicle Routing Needs Social Awareness for Coexistence with Human Drivers
- arxiv_id: 2610.13439v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Anastasia Psarou, Mikolaj Rams, Blazej Torbus, Rafal Kucharski
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13439v1

Collective Autonomous Vehicle (AV) route choices in real-world traffic networks can create externalities for Human-Driven Vehicles (HDVs), as routes that benefit AVs may increase travel times for other road users. To address this, we introduce a social component, the marginal cost, to the selfish travel time AV reward. Marginal cost captures an AV's impact on other road users by comparing their travel times with those in a counterfactual simulation in which the AV is removed. We study AV route choice in mixed-autonomy traffic settings using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), where AVs learn routing policies through repeated interactions with the traffic environment. Experiments on the real-world networks of Ingolstadt and Provins show that incorporating marginal cost into the AV reward not only limits the increase in HDV travel times and shortens AV travel times relative to selfish AV routing, but also improves overall system performance. In Ingolstadt, selfish routing of 40\% AVs increases the travel times of other vehicles by 1.7\%, and this drops to 1.1\% when the social reward is included. Interestingly, not only does overall system performance improve, but the AVs are also better off when acting socially.This effect is consistent across tested cities, penetration rates, and replications, yet when the weight of the social component in the reward increases, the system fails to reach the desired performance. For a more scalable approach to social AV routing in mixed-autonomy traffic, we compute the marginal cost only for the AVs whose route choices most affect the traffic system. This reduces the number of counterfactual simulations and achieves similar performance at a significantly lower computational cost. Our results suggest that AV routing should go beyond selfish rewards and, for the benefit of all traffic participants, include a social component in its objective.

## When AI Advisors Agree: Distinguishing Forecast Risk from Forecast Reliability
- arxiv_id: 2610.13431v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Russlan Ramdowar
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13431v1

If seven AI advisors give similar forecasts, should we trust them more? Agreement can indicate useful shared evidence, but it can also reflect an asset that was easier to forecast in the first place. We examine this distinction using 928 completed first-quarter forecasts for 232 assets across four production batches of iPulse AI. Ten historical Gemini configurations are grouped into seven investment frameworks; their archived forecasts are compared with later market outcomes, without generating new reports. Mean absolute return error rises from 10.31 percentage points in the lowest-disagreement quartile to 21.05 in the highest. Yet a forecast of no price change shows almost the same increase, from 9.89 to 21.15. The relationship between disagreement and additional forecast error is not resolved after accounting for visible asset risk. Retaining the quarter of forecasts with lowest historical volatility produces lower error than retaining those with least disagreement: 7.97 versus 10.31 percentage points. Agreement about direction behaves differently. When at least six of seven frameworks share a direction, directional accuracy is higher, but return-magnitude error is also larger. These results distinguish three questions often collapsed into one confidence score: how risky the asset is, whether advisors agree on its direction, and how precisely they forecast its return. For this historical council, agreement is informative in specific ways; it is not a general certificate of forecast reliability.

## The Limits of Manipulation within Personalized Incentive Policies
- arxiv_id: 2610.13133v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Andrea Araldo, Alessa Mayer, Ravi Seshadri, Amir Brudner
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13133v1

Demand management policies aim to shift individual behavior toward sustainable choices (e.g., goods, transport modes). Personalized incentive policies tailor subsidies to agents' needs and constraints, but personalization also exposes the regulator to agents' strategic manipulative behavior. The extent to which agents can profitably manipulate such policies remains poorly understood, which limits their applicability. This paper establishes analytically the conditions under which manipulation benefits the agent. We formalize a Stackelberg game in which an agent may simulate a different profile to extract higher incentives from a regulator. Previous work typically assumes that the regulator would instantaneously classify agents into the simulated user profile. In contrast, we explicitly model the regulator's process of learning the agent profile, based on observed past behavior. This makes manipulation harder, since it forces the agent to consistently behave according to a simulated profile, thus renouncing following their true preferences. Manipulative agents thus incur a Cost of Manipulation, which has typically been neglected. By accounting for it, we get a counterintuitive finding: although classification uncertainty reduces personalization accuracy, it may benefit the regulator by deterring manipulation. We establish an upper bound on the agent's gain from manipulation, which becomes stricter as profiles become harder to distinguish for the regulator. Simulations on the London Passenger Mode Choice dataset illustrate this deterrence effect. The framework can therefore be used to stress-test the robustness of personalized incentive policies before deployment.

## The value of information and the emergence of communication in evolved e-puck robots
- arxiv_id: 2610.13118v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Fernando Montes-Gonzalez
- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13118v1

We study when communication emerges between autonomous agents and what role an innate action selection architecture plays. Two e-puck robots, reconstructed in PyBullet from their Webots model and validated against it, must find a feeding zone that can only be detected when a robot passes over it. Each robot can emit a one-bit signal whose direction is detected by the receiver. Control is provided by a GRU recurrent network with a residual path, either operating alone or as a channel within an Embedded Action Selection Architecture (EASA). Fitness measures only the time the robots spend over the food. When the robots receive a compass pointing to the food, use of the signal disappears completely. Within EASA, innate reflexes constrain the network and reduce performance. As its priorities evolve, evolution consistently weakens those reflexes. The results suggest that communication emerges in proportion to the value of the information it transmits, and that an innate scaffolding designed for isolated agents can be an impediment in a social task.

## SwingGuard: Risk-Conditioned Multi-Agent Swing Trading with Dual-Timescale Governance
- arxiv_id: 2610.12988v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA

- published: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-09
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12988v1

Cryptocurrency swing trading spans mismatched timescales: positions remain active while market and event risk can change within minutes. However, many predictive and large language model (LLM)-based trading systems assess risk after strategy generation or run both functions at the same cadence, leaving proposals unconditioned by current risk and exposure weakly governed between revisions. We propose SwingGuard, a dual-timescale, long-only multi-agent framework for risk-conditioned strategy generation. At the strategy timescale, specialized analysts synthesize point-in-time technical, sentiment, on-chain, and macroeconomic evidence through bullish-bearish deliberation. The resulting evidence and current risk context are then combined by a Portfolio Architect to form a target position. At the governance timescale, a five-minute Risk Controller performs conditional diagnosis, maintains persistent risk states with staged recovery, audits the proposal, and constrains execution until the next strategy revision. The exposure-capped joint portfolio achieves a Sharpe ratio of 2.98, compared with 2.79 for an otherwise identical Post-hoc Risk variant. Structured traces further link risk evidence and state transitions to strategy constraints and executed position changes, providing an auditable framework for asynchronous cryptocurrency swing trading. Overall, the results indicate that conditioning strategy formation on current risk, together with persistent high-frequency governance, improves risk-adjusted performance without sacrificing auditability.

In this work, we show that a reward-hacking strategy elicited in one agent can propagate to others and affect system coordination. ## When Agent Context Goes Stale: Incoherence in Volatile Agent Context

- authors: Yingying Liu, Junzhou Fang, Chenxiong Qian
- published: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-06
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05281v2

Modern agents increasingly ground their reasoning in observations returned by tools, such as file contents read from a workspace. However, the data sources underlying these observations may later be modified by users, other agents, or external tools, while the model retains only the stale content in its context window. Existing agent runtimes provide little support for notifying the model that a previously observed fact has become stale, causing agents to reuse outdated observations and make incorrect claims about the current workspace state. We propose Concord, a context coherence framework that maintains the consistency between tool observation in agent context and the mutable sources from which they were derived. Concord links each observation to its source, detects source changes, and uses configurable handling policies to update, annotate, or suppress stale context before reuse. Concord is applicable across different agent runtimes and external resources, and can be easily extended to new runtime-resource settings. We implement Concord as a general framework, and instantiate a concrete use case to assess its effectiveness. We construct ConcordBench, where previously observed file contents become stale after subsequent edits. Across three evaluated frontier models, Concord produces answers consistent with the restored workspace state in all evaluated cases under these constructed conditions, matching the oracle on recover count for this benchmark, while using 46.4% fewer tokens than the strongest non-oracle baseline.

## PRA-TLS: Attestation of a Client Application for TEE
- arxiv_id: 2610.06061v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Reina Sasaki, Yutaka Ishikawa, Atsuko Takefusa, Masato Oguchi
- published: 2026-10-05 · updated: 2026-10-05
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06061v1

Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs) are secure foundations for protecting sensitive information and executing computations over confidential data. Processing in an isolated execution environment (enclave) is invoked by an untrusted application in the Rich Execution Environment (REE). Then the enclave returns the execution results to the untrusted application. Even if the enclave has been attested, the boundary between the enclave and the untrusted application poses risks, such as tampering with input arguments or return values and manipulating the order in which the application invokes functions. Therefore, it is necessary to establish the authenticity and integrity of not only the enclave itself but also the application and its execution environment. In this study, we propose an attestation protocol, Portable Remote Attestation TLS (PRA-TLS), for a client application that invokes an enclave. We introduce a daemon that acts as an attester. PRA-TLS uses a trusted Attester Daemon to measure the state of the host environment and application code at runtime, providing these measurements as attestation evidence for verification by a remote Verifier. This mechanism allows the enclave to proceed only when the software environment is in the expected state and the application code is verified as legitimate. We define attack models and security requirements for the proposed protocol and formally evaluate its security using the Tamarin Prover. Furthermore, we implement a prototype of PRA-TLS using Intel SGX and evaluate its performance.

## No More Translation at Runtime: LLM-Empowered Static Binary Translation
- arxiv_id: 2610.05999v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Zhibo Liu, Huaijin Wang, Wai Kin Wong, Daoyuan Wu, Shuai Wang
- published: 2026-10-05 · updated: 2026-10-05
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05999v1

While AArch64 CPUs are becoming strong market contenders, their software ecosystem lags behind the mature x86-64 environment, hindering the adoption of the new architectures and impacting user experience. Binary translation bridges this divide by converting binary code from one architecture (e.g., x86-64) to run on another (e.g., AArch64), allowing legacy software to benefit from modern hardware's performance and energy efficiency advantages. Current translation methods are typically either dynamic, which adds significant runtime overhead, or static, which struggles with reliability due to the inherent complexities of binary analysis. This paper introduces a new static, assembly-to-assembly translation paradigm that transforms binary code ahead of execution, generating portable, efficient native-like binaries that run on AArch64 devices without runtime frameworks. Benefiting from recent breakthroughs in large language models (LLMs), we provide a practical and automated translation engine that produces high-quality code with minimal human intervention. To ensure correctness, we introduce a crucial verification step, where we split the assembly code into simplified snippets, enabling efficient and scalable semantic verification. Our evaluation shows that this approach significantly outperforms existing open-source solutions with a large margin, producing binaries with near-native performance. Furthermore, it shows substantial improvements over the leading industrial translator, ExaGear, illuminating a promising new direction for cross-architecture binary translation research.

## FDP: The Data Placement Promise of Modern NVMe SSDs
- arxiv_id: 2610.02676v2 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Sijie Lan, Hui Qi, Xing He, Mahmut Kandemir, Javier González, Vivek Shah
- published: 2026-10-02 · updated: 2026-10-05
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02676v2

NVMe SSDs are now widely deployed as the storage tier in data centers. As SSDs have evolved over the past decade, the commu- nity has continued to debate the interfaces they expose and how operating systems and storage systems should exploit them. The NVMe Flexible Data Placement (FDP) proposal is the latest point in this design space. FDP introduces an interface based on Reclaim Units that enables explicit data placement to reduce device write amplification without the software engineering costs of sequential- write constraints and host garbage collection. FDP-enabled SSDs are emerging in commercial products and early data center de- ployments. Their compatibility with conventional block I/O allows existing applications to run unchanged, allowing a frictionless adop- tion in industry. This paper presents an experimental evaluation of FDP SSDs to characterize their data placement guarantees over the raw device interface. We then revisit two widely deployed and distinct open- source storage systems, MySQL and RocksDB, and examine whether lifetime-based data separation and distinct write patterns built into their architectures can be mapped onto FDP SSDs without inva- sive changes. Our evaluation shows end-to-end WAF reductions at higher device utilization, along with QoS and throughput improve- ments under synthetic and real-world workloads. These results demonstrate that FDP provides a practical and deployable cross- layer mechanism for data placement with open-source ecosystem support on Linux. They also highlight why FDP SSDs are gaining traction in industry.

## RetainZ: Reclaim-Time-Aware Placement for AI Checkpoints on Zoned SSDs
- arxiv_id: 2610.05554v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Minxing Chu, Ruoxi Yang
- published: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-04
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05554v1

Solid State Drives (SSDs) are becoming the dominant medium for performance-critical storage. Zoned Namespace (ZNS) SSDs are getting more and more attractive because sequential writes and explicit zone resets reduce address-mapping, over-provisioning, and internal garbage-collection costs. However, reclaiming space requires resetting an entire zone, so deleting one file does not free its space while other files there must be kept. This erase-before-write constraint becomes a bottleneck for write-intensive workloads, particularly AI checkpointing during model training, where frequent saves of model and optimizer state compete for space with long-retained checkpoints. We observe that retention policies inherently encode when data can be retired, avoiding the need for a learned lifetime predictor. We present RetainZ, a storage backend that translates these policies into explicit reclaim epochs. Its allocator isolates long-retained data and groups objects with nearby reclaim epochs, balancing delayed space reclamation against unused space at zone ends. Evaluated on FEMU with multi-tenant streams and checkpoint manifests up to Pythia-1B, RetainZ reduces space occupied by deleted data and unused zone tails by 41.7% and mean per-input checkpoint p99 latency by 47.5% at 84% target load compared with the allocator of ZenFS, an established lifetime-aware ZNS backend, and lowers host write amplification from 1.165 to 1.003.

## Optimal Dynamic Resource Allocation for Multicore Real-time Systems
- arxiv_id: 2610.05530v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Georgiy A. Bondar, Abigail Eisenklam, Ripon C. Sarker, Linh Thi Xuan Phan, Abhishek Halder
- published: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-04
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05530v1

We formulate a novel optimal control problem for data-driven dynamic resource allocation in multicore real-time systems. The proposed formulation accounts for hard deadline constraints and resource bounds. Under mild assumptions, we prove several results on the feasibility and existence of optimal solution for the proposed formulation. Building on these analyses, we design an algorithm to solve the multi-core dynamic resource allocation problem. We demonstrate the practical use of this new algorithm by experimentally evaluating it on a real multicore platform using four benchmarks from the literature.

## Awkernel: RTOS Bridging DAG Scheduling Theory and Component-Oriented Real-Time Systems Practice
- arxiv_id: 2610.04861v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Atsushi Yano, Ryo Nishimura, Yutaro Kobayashi, Yuuki Takano, Takahiro Ishikawa-Aso, Takuya Azumi
- published: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-04
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04861v1

Real-time directed acyclic graph (DAG) scheduling provides a rigorous basis for timing analysis and efficiency in component-oriented real-time systems (CORTS) such as autonomous driving systems, yet challenges remain from the perspectives of both theorists and practitioners. For theorists, no OS natively supports the DAG task model; algorithms are therefore evaluated only analytically or in simulation, ignoring real-hardware overheads. For practitioners, today's CORTS platforms (ROS~2 and AUTOSAR Adaptive Platform) are designed for service composition rather than timing analyzability; adopting DAG scheduling research therefore demands substantial code changes and developer discipline. This paper proposes Awkernel, the first open-source real-time operating system (RTOS) that natively supports real-time DAG scheduling research, bridging DAG scheduling theory and CORTS practice. For theorists, Awkernel is a real-hardware testbed: it exposes a modular OS-native DAG scheduler for easy algorithm implementation, plus a random workload generator and built-in metrics for evaluation. For practitioners, Awkernel is not a full replacement for today's platforms but a CORTS platform maximizing timing analyzability: its familiar publish/subscribe model keeps porting mechanical, mapping each callback to one subtask. Moreover, its function-as-subtask (FasS) API enforces DAG semantics (precedence constraints) without programmer discipline, yielding applications that match the theoretical DAG model and directly inherit its efficiency and safety guarantees. On a Raspberry Pi~4, microbenchmarks confirm microsecond-level scheduling overheads; a global earliest deadline first (GEDF) scheduler fits in 226~lines; and a practitioner ports part of an autonomous driving system using only the FasS API, without reasoning about DAG semantics.
