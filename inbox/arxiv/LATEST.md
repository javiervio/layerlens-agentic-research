## LesionAgent: Training-Free Agentic Tumor Tracking and Response Assessment in Longitudinal CT
- arxiv_id: 2611.27876v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Maximilian Rokuss, Jeya Maria Jose Valanarasu, Alejandro Lozano, Benjamin Hamm, Yannick Kirchhoff, Fabian Isensee et al.
- published: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-20
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27876v1

Every few months, a cancer patient's CT scans decide whether treatment continues. 3D foundation models can now detect, segment, register and track lesions, yet none of them reads the scans as a radiologist does. A radiologist decides which findings are lesions, which are the same lesion months later and whether a new spot means progression, and checks each decision against the extensive rules of a clinical guideline. We introduce LesionAgent, an LLM agent that performs this read end to end without task-specific training. It operates frozen 3D models as tools, acts in 3D through coordinate-labelled views, remembers every lesion across visits in a registry, verifies every model output it relies on, and leaves every number to a deterministic guideline engine. On four public longitudinal CT datasets, LesionAgent tracks more lesions and measures them more accurately than every automatic baseline, and where the same 3D models chained by fixed rules call progression falsely at 33 of 56 follow-ups, it does so twice. On clinical follow-up CTs, it agrees with the radiologists' response at 20 of 23 follow-ups, including every change in response, and without training on DeepLesion, it retrieves more of its lesions than every tracker trained on it (89.0% vs. 87.4%). Its harness, not its LLM, makes the difference: given the same LLM and 3D models but not the harness, Claude Code reaches a detection F1 of 41% and LesionAgent 78%, and an open-weight LLM that can run inside the hospital rises from 6% to 73%. LesionAgent and a napari reading app for any LLM are available at https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/LesionAgent.

## Beyond Self-Critique: Epistemic Convergence Control for Multi-Agent Research Verification
- arxiv_id: 2611.27839v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Chrisantus Eze
- published: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-20
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27839v1

Multi-agent systems for scientific claim verification increasingly rely on role-structured LLM debate to pressure-test hypotheses. However, when debating agents are instantiated from the same underlying base model, they share identical latent representations, making self-critique bias structurally inevitable. Static fixed-iteration protocols compound this by either halting prematurely before evidence stabilizes or wasting inference compute after convergence. We present \sys{}, a multi-agent research verification framework that addresses both failure modes. First, we introduce an asymmetric role architecture comprising an Advocate, Skeptic, and Arbitrator with opposing epistemic directives, producing significantly lower role-embedding similarity (0.852 vs.\ 0.918 for symmetric Multi-Agent Debate [MAD], $p = 5.82 \times 10^{-8}$) and surfacing more contested claims (22.0\% vs.\ 15.4\%). Second, we propose the Belief-Delta ($\DB$) convergence criterion, an evidence-grounded stopping rule that eliminates over-iteration (0.0\% vs.\ 62.5\% for Fixed-$3$) while cutting LLM calls by 2.4$\times$. Third, each supported claim is anchored to a verbatim literature snippet audited by an independent NLI classifier, achieving a 21.7\% hallucination rate (comparable to 25.9\% for a Self-Refine critic, $p = 0.61$) while establishing auditable evidence grounding. Across 200 runs on 40 research hypotheses evaluated with DeepSeek-R1-32B, \sys{} balances compute efficiency with rigorous epistemic verification.

## Where Delay Compensation Belongs in a Decentralized Mean-Field-Game Swarm with a Control-Barrier Safety Filter
- arxiv_id: 2611.27540v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA
- authors: Jan Mikołajczyk, Igor Rostkowski, Bartosz Ogórkiewicz, Szymon Skierski, Sylwia Opat, Wojciech Rybak et al.
- published: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-20
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27540v1

Large robot teams are often run as a plan-then-execute stack. We study a decentralized instance for drone swarms, a mean-field-game (MFG) routing field with a per-agent control-barrier-function (CBF) safety filter, when communication is limited by a shared TDMA channel. Neighbors are known only through noisy range and bearing measurements exchanged in round-robin slots, so the frame, and with it the age of every estimate, grows with swarm size. In simulation this delay reaches both layers but breaks only the safety layer. Without delay, swarms of up to 100 agents do not collide, even with each measurement's noise held for a whole frame; with it, stale estimates at the CBF cause collisions beyond a frame period of 125 to 175 ms, or 75 to 100 ms at 100 agents with held noise, and swarm size sets where that limit lies and how steeply collisions rise. At the MFG, staleness makes the local density noisier without biasing it, and throughput drops by at most a tenth. We then compensate with lightweight on-board predictors, from constant-velocity fits to a small learned network, that estimate each neighbor's position from its last broadcasts. A layer-split experiment shows where prediction is needed: at the CBF it restores safety and extends the tolerated frame period by 1.4 to 1.7 times with an untrained constant-velocity fit and about 2.3 with the learned network when the noise is redrawn each step; with noise held for a frame the two extend it about equally, while at the MFG it only refines the congestion correction. A constant wider safety margin, the simplest alternative, makes collisions more frequent in a bottleneck. With the routing solved offline, that layer is robust to the delay because it sees neighbors only through a crowd average; compensation belongs in the safety filter, switched on only above the uncompensated limit, since below it the predictor's own error adds collisions.

- arxiv_id: 2611.27368v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.MA

- published: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-20
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27368v1

Deep parallel cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (CMARL)} trains multiple agents that all act at every time step to optimize a common objective. Like single-agent RL, CMARL can produce unsafe behaviors that violate implicit safety requirements not captured by the reward, and these behaviors are hard to explain. However, testing CMARL policies for safety has received less attention than the safety testing of single-agent RL policies. ## Model Carving: Training Transformers That Optimize Their Own Topology
- arxiv_id: 2611.27962v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.AI
- authors: Kaden Zheng, Pratyusha Sharma
- published: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-20
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27962v1

Machine learning has largely kept separate the choice of architecture and the process of training. A classic Transformer, for instance, trains a fixed set of attention and MLP sublayers at every layer, in the same order, and optimizes the weights within. This uniformity is a convention rather than a principled design choice, and it is likely suboptimal: the best architecture for a given task may be smaller, more efficient, and far from uniform. We believe that, ideally, training itself should shape the model's topology. To this end, we introduce Model Carving, which learns both a Transformer's weights and its architecture in a single run. By applying learnable, scalar gates to each sublayer, we allow the training loss to remove the model's own components during training -- freeing a smaller heterogeneous network inside. We evaluate Model Carving by pre-training standard Transformers from 140M to 2.7B parameters and looped Transformers from 140M to 1.3B. At equal training compute, carved models reach a lower validation loss than dense models trained with up to 1.7 times more compute. Furthermore, carved models also outperform size-matched models produced by pruning or distillation, suggesting that the best way to obtain small models for edge devices is to carve them during training, not to compress a large one afterwards. This advantage both grows with scale and carries over to downstream tasks such as few-shot evaluation and generation. Because the removed sublayers no longer run, carved models also cost up to 34% fewer FLOPs per token and decode up to 1.6 times faster without cost in quality. Our results show that architecture, like weights, should be learned and not fixed in advance, and that inside every large transformer is a smaller, faster, stronger one waiting to be carved out.

On two page-fault-heavy workloads, Overseer reduces runtime by roughly 35% on Hilbert-curve traversal and by roughly an order of magnitude on dense matrix multiplication, with corresponding reductions in major-fault counts.

## Polymorph: Operating System Kernel Optimization Through Artificial Intelligence
- arxiv_id: 2611.23644v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Dean Sysman, Georgios Liargkovas, Tal Zussman, Hasan Zengin, Petra Hedesiu, Hubertus Franke et al.
- published: 2026-11-18 · updated: 2026-11-18
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23644v1

While popular operating systems such as Linux and Windows were originally designed as monolithic kernels, an increasing number of kernel subsystems have become extensible using eBPF. Users can now define custom policies for essential kernel mechanisms, such as the process scheduler, page cache, and network stack, without recompiling the kernel. These mechanisms give developers significant freedom to fit the kernel to their applications' workloads, but the question of which policy to deploy remains open. We study how to use agentic AI to automate kernel policy development, selection, and deployment, focusing on the page cache and the process scheduler as two representative subsystems. We show that existing approaches fall short: evolutionary and agentic search runs offline, is relatively expensive, and often fails to generalize to online workloads, while online knob tuners are encumbered by the knobs' lack of expressivity. In contrast, we propose Polymorph, which learns online: an AI agent writes a generalized, granular policy once, and a user mode tuner adjusts its parameters during runtime, with no LLM in the loop. For the page cache, Polymorph improves throughput over Linux's LRU by 8.4% on unseen data, nearly twice the gain of the best existing policy, while adapting to workload changes at runtime and costing only 16M tokens once, versus 125-240M tokens per workload for agentic offline search. For the process scheduler, it improves on Linux's default by 2.75x across six workloads, versus 2.27x for the best fixed sched_ext policy.

## Towards Fair Resource Sharing in Agentic LLM Serving
- arxiv_id: 2611.23558v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Sehyeon Lee, Yuchen Xia, Zhaoyang Hong, Jinrui Hu, Anuradha Pandey, Evan Zhao et al.
- published: 2026-11-17 · updated: 2026-11-17
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23558v1

As LLM agents increasingly execute complex, multi-step tasks on shared inference infrastructure, resource contention across the end-to-end LLM serving stack causes severe and uneven slowdowns across agents. Existing agentic serving systems primarily optimize throughput and latency, with comparatively limited attention to fairness among concurrent agents. Although fairness has been studied in conventional LLM serving at the token and request levels, these approaches do not fully capture cumulative delays and stateful resource dependencies across successive LLM calls within an agent. Moreover, interference spans request routing, runtime scheduling, and hardware resources, making agent-level fairness challenging to define and optimize across an end-to-end serving stack. This paper presents a cross-layer framework to define agent-level fairness, attribute sources of unfairness, and optimize fairness while preserving throughput. To account for agents with widely varying execution demands, we build a theoretical foundation to define fairness using slowdown relative to standalone execution. Our empirical analysis reveals that routing, runtime scheduling, and hardware contention jointly determine fairness, with performance-oriented policies often leading to prolonged queuing delays. To optimize fairness, we introduce Duru, which monitors each agent's slowdown and uses this signal to guide admission and scheduling decisions across the router and runtime. Duru further alleviates starvation through admission exceptions and integrates slowdown-driven autoscaling to adhere to Service-Level Objectives (SLOs). Our evaluation across various benchmarks and hardware platforms shows that Duru reduces 99th-percentile normalized slowdown by a geometric mean of 44.4% (up to 79.5%) while delivering 91.8-127.4% of the state-of-the-art system's throughput.

## Restoring Fair Hrtick Re-arming After Same-Task Repicks in the Linux EEVDF Scheduler
- arxiv_id: 2611.19893v1 · primary surfaced via: cs.OS
- authors: Shubhang Kaushik
- published: 2026-11-15 · updated: 2026-11-15
- link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19893v1

Linux uses one-shot high-resolution scheduler ticks (hrticks) to request rescheduling at fair-class execution boundaries. We analyze a gap in the dispatch path in which an expired fair hrtick is not restarted when the fair scheduler selects the current task again. The gap arises because the same-task fast path bypasses set_next_task_fair(), the location that normally records the next fair hrtick delay. We introduce SNT_REPICK, an explicit same-task activation type for struct sched_class. For fair tasks, it preserves the fast path while restarting the hrtick from refreshed EEVDF state. For SCHED_DEADLINE, it deliberately performs no hrtick restart because the corresponding runtime state is not refreshed on a repick. The implementation was merged into the mainline Linux kernel as commit c72945693b. We give a conditional timing analysis for systems on which the periodic scheduler tick remains active and report validation from two platforms. Functional tests on an 80-core Ampere Altra exercised fair hrtick, deadline hrtick, and the CONFIG_HIGH_RES_TIMERS=n fallback. Independently, Intel's 0-Day Kernel Test Service reported a 34.2% increase in its stress-ng.rawudp.packets_32_bytes_received_per_sec metric on a 192-thread Intel Xeon 6740E under the documented test configuration. We distinguish this reported benchmark result from causal claims that require separate scheduler and network instrumentation.
