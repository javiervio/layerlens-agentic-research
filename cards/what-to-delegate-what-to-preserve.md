## Claims from this source
- C-0144: A scenario-based survey of 289 U.S. adults with prior AI experience, covering 45 use cases across nine life domains (one scenario per domain per respondent), measured automation preference, trust, collaboration, AI leadership, privacy concern, and willingness to pay. Automation preferences varied substantially across domains; greater trust, desired AI leadership, prior task-specific AI use, and anticipated financial savings were associated with greater automation preference, while concern that automation would reduce personal fulfillment predicted lower preference. Open-ended responses revealed a preference for "bounded autonomy": participants often favored substantial AI execution while retaining human involvement, oversight, or final authority. The authors argue delegation is best understood as a configuration of execution, collaboration, leadership, and authority, not a single autonomy dial.
